Most systems fail not because they lack capability, but because they accumulated capability without discipline. Every feature added is a future decision deferred. Every option preserved is clarity sacrificed.

The instinct in organizations is to add. Add a dashboard. Add a workflow. Add an approval step. The logic is always reasonable in isolation. The cumulative effect is always paralysis.

Simplicity requires a different kind of courage. Not the courage to build — anyone can build. The courage to refuse. To look at a perfectly functional feature and say: this does not earn its place.

I learned this on mountains before I learned it in boardrooms. At altitude, every gram matters. You do not pack for comfort. You pack for survival. The question is never "might this be useful?" The question is "will I die without it?"

Software, programs, and organizations deserve the same ruthlessness. Not because minimalism is aesthetically pleasing — though it is — but because every unnecessary element is a surface area for failure. Every unused feature is a maintenance burden. Every redundant process is a tax on attention.

The discipline is not in the cutting. The discipline is in knowing what to keep.
